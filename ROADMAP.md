# Engineering by Intuition — Roadmap

## Phase 0 — First Learning Experience

### Goal

Build and launch the first small learning experience that demonstrates the Engineering by Intuition teaching philosophy.

The first learning path will introduce differential equations through the question:

> **How can mathematics describe the way real systems change with time?**

### First Learning Path

**Differential Equations — From Reality to Engineering**

#### Chapter 1 — Change Is Everywhere

Introduce change and rate of change through familiar physical phenomena.

Focus on developing curiosity about:

* What is changing?
* How quickly is it changing?
* Does the rate of change remain constant?
* What determines the rate?

No formal differential equation is introduced initially.

---

#### Chapter 2 — Why Does Hot Tea Cool Down?

Observe the cooling of a hot object.

Develop intuition for:

* Temperature difference
* Equilibrium
* Fast versus slow change
* Why cooling slows down
* Why the temperature approaches room temperature

The learner makes qualitative predictions before seeing the mathematics.

---

#### Chapter 3 — From Intuition to a Differential Equation

Translate the physical intuition into mathematics.

Introduce:

* Rate of change
* Derivatives
* First-order differential equations
* Newton's law of cooling
* Physical meaning of each term
* Assumptions behind the model

The emphasis is on understanding where the equation comes from rather than memorizing it.

---

#### Chapter 4 — Can We Predict the Future?

Use the differential equation to predict how temperature changes with time.

Introduce:

* Solving the equation
* Exponential behavior
* Time constant
* Initial conditions
* Long-term behavior

The learner compares mathematical predictions with physical intuition.

---

#### Chapter 5 — Let's Build the Experiment

Create an interactive simulation of the cooling system.

The learner should be able to:

* Change initial temperature
* Change room temperature
* Change cooling rate
* Predict the outcome
* Run the simulation
* Compare prediction with result
* Explore the effect of parameters

The simulation acts as a virtual laboratory.

---

#### Chapter 6 — One Idea, Many Systems

Show that the same mathematical structure appears in different physical and engineering systems.

Examples include:

* Cooling systems
* RC circuits
* RL circuits
* First-order control systems
* Other first-order dynamical systems

The goal is to recognize the common mathematical structure beneath different physical systems.

---

## MVP Launch Criteria

The first public version should contain:

* At least 4 polished written chapters
* One complete learning path
* At least 1 interactive simulation
* Basic navigation between chapters
* Mathematical notation rendered clearly
* Mobile-friendly layout
* A simple, clean interface
* Public deployment

Chapters beyond the minimum can be added before launch if they reach the required quality.

Quality is more important than chapter count.

---

## After the First Learning Path

The next stage will expand the differential-equation journey:

**First-order systems**

→ **Second-order systems**

→ **Mass-spring system**

→ **Damping**

→ **Forced systems**

→ **Resonance**

→ **Coupled systems**

→ **State-space**

→ **Control systems**

The same ideas will then be connected to mathematics, physics, electrical engineering, electronics, software, and eventually quantum mechanics and quantum computing.

---

## Development Philosophy

The platform will evolve through short cycles:

**Build → Publish → Observe → Improve → Build**

We will prefer creating a small number of excellent learning experiences over producing a large amount of content quickly.

The website architecture should evolve alongside the content rather than being over-engineered before the learning experience is proven.

---

## Long-Term Direction

Engineering by Intuition will gradually become a connected learning platform where concepts from different disciplines reinforce each other.

The long-term objective is not simply to provide lessons.

It is to help learners develop the ability to:

**Observe → Question → Intuit → Predict → Formalize → Experiment → Apply → Reflect**

and ultimately become comfortable reasoning about unfamiliar systems from first principles.
