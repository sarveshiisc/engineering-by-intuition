# Engineering by Intuition

## Vision

Engineering by Intuition is a free learning platform for understanding science and engineering from first principles.

The goal is not to teach students how to memorize equations.

The goal is to help them understand **why the equations exist in the first place**.

We start with the physical world, develop intuition, translate that intuition into mathematics, experiment through simulation, and connect the ideas to real engineering systems.

---

## The Learning Cycle

Every major concept should follow a similar journey:

**Observe → Question → Intuit → Predict → Mathematics → Simulate → Apply → Reflect**

### Observe

Start with a real phenomenon, physical system, experiment, or problem.

The learner should see something that happens in the real world before being introduced to the mathematical description of it.

### Question

Turn observation into curiosity.

Ask:

* Why does this happen?
* What determines its behavior?
* What would happen if we changed something?
* Can we predict what happens next?

Questions are more important than formulas at the beginning.

### Intuit

Build a mental model of what is happening.

Use words, physical reasoning, diagrams, animations, and simple thought experiments.

The learner should develop an understanding of the behavior before formal mathematics is introduced.

### Predict

Use the developing intuition to make a prediction.

For example:

> If we increase this parameter, what do you think will happen?

The prediction should be testable.

This creates a cycle of:

**Intuition → Prediction → Experiment → Correction → Better Intuition**

### Mathematics

Once the physical behavior is understood intuitively, introduce the mathematics that describes it.

Mathematics should feel like a language for expressing an idea that the learner already understands.

The goal is not simply to present an equation, but to explain:

* Where it comes from
* What each term represents physically
* Why it has that particular form
* What assumptions were made

### Simulate

Use computation as a laboratory.

Allow the learner to change parameters, run experiments, visualize results, and compare them with predictions.

Simulation should encourage experimentation rather than simply display predetermined animations.

### Apply

Transfer the idea to a different situation.

The learner should discover where the same underlying concept or mathematical structure appears elsewhere.

For example:

**Cooling of a hot object**

→ **RC circuit**

→ **RL circuit**

→ **Drug concentration**

→ **First-order control system**

The objective is to recognize common structure beneath apparently different physical systems.

### Reflect

Step back from the mathematics and ask:

> **Do I actually understand what is happening?**

Reflection should test the learner's mental model rather than simply their ability to manipulate equations.

A learner should be able to:

* Explain the phenomenon in their own words
* Predict how the system will respond to changes
* Explain what the mathematical terms mean physically
* Identify assumptions and limitations
* Explain the concept without relying entirely on formulas

---

## Start With Reality

Instead of beginning with:

> "Here is the equation. Memorize it."

we begin with:

> **"Here is something that happens in the real world. Why does it happen?"**

For example, instead of introducing a differential equation as a formula, we might begin with a cup of hot tea cooling down.

Why does it cool?

Why does it cool faster initially?

Why does the cooling slow down?

Why does it approach room temperature?

Only after developing a physical understanding do we introduce the differential equation that describes the behavior.

---

## Mathematics Is a Language

Mathematics should not feel like an obstacle placed between the learner and understanding.

It should feel like a language that allows us to describe what we already understand intuitively.

The learner should repeatedly experience:

> **"I understand what is happening physically — now I can express it mathematically."**

At the same time, mathematics should eventually become a tool for discovering things that are difficult or impossible to see directly.

Intuition and mathematics should strengthen each other.

---

## Simulation Is the Laboratory

Interactive simulations should allow learners to experiment.

Change a parameter.

Observe what happens.

Make a prediction.

Run the simulation.

Compare the result with the prediction.

Ask why the result occurred.

This turns learning from passive consumption into experimentation.

---

## From Fundamentals to Engineering

Concepts should connect to real systems and to other disciplines.

For example:

**Differential equations**

→ Dynamical systems

→ Oscillations

→ Electrical circuits

→ Mechanical systems

→ Signals

→ Control systems

→ State-space models

The same mathematical ideas should repeatedly appear in different physical contexts.

This helps learners recognize underlying structures rather than seeing every subject as a collection of unrelated formulas.

---

## Long-Term Scope

Engineering by Intuition will gradually expand across:

* Mathematics
* Physics
* Electrical Engineering
* Electronics
* Control Systems
* Signals and Systems
* Software and Computing
* Quantum Mechanics
* Quantum Computing

The platform should reveal connections between these subjects rather than keeping them isolated.

---

## The Standard

Every lesson should leave the learner with something more valuable than a formula:

> **A mental model.**

If a learner can:

1. Explain why something happens
2. Predict what will happen when conditions change
3. Express the behavior mathematically
4. Test the model through simulation or experiment
5. Apply the idea to another system
6. Identify the assumptions and limitations

then the lesson has succeeded.

---

## Our Principle

**Don't start with equations. Start with reality.**

**Understand first. Predict second. Formalize third. Experiment fourth. Apply everywhere.**

And above all:

> **Build intuition that survives the mathematics.**
